# 07 — AI Integration Strategy

This document records how PMAS will integrate AI / healthcare-intelligence capabilities — including what PMAS will deliberately **not** do. It distils a senior-architecture review (September 2026) of integrating PMAS with a separate Healthcare-AI repository (Flask + RandomForest symptom prediction + rule-based chatbot).

## The five adopted principles

From the review, these principles govern all AI work in PMAS:

1. **PMAS owns the patient journey and longitudinal record.** AI services own interpretation only.
2. **Integration happens through a versioned health-data contract** (JSON, schema-versioned — see [doc 03](03-data-model.md)), never through shared databases, CSV paths, or internal function calls.
3. **A safety layer sits between any AI output and the patient.** No probabilities, no diagnoses, no unreviewed medical claims, no model confidences shown to patients.
4. **AI must degrade gracefully.** If the intelligence service is unavailable, PMAS continues full functioning — offline-first is the guarantee.
5. **Structured knowledge is the source of truth; generative models explain it.** Retrieval-grounded, not free-generating.

## What PMAS will NOT build

| Rejected capability | Reason |
|---|---|
| Patient-facing disease prediction with confidence scores | Regulatory exposure (symptom-checkers can fall under medical-device rules), clinical liability, and trust risk. A "94% Dengue" message to a patient is a clinical claim. |
| A general-purpose medical chatbot | High hallucination risk with no pharmacist gate; not the thesis problem. |
| Real-time LLM callouts in the patient critical path | Per-call cost; failure mode inside the core loop; unnecessary for adherence. |
| Microservices/gateway/event-bus infrastructure "for AI" | One developer, one pilot. Complexity is a cost without a patient benefit at this scale. |
| Training models on patient data for prediction | DPDP consent covers care + de-identified research, not exploratory model training. Any future modelling happens on Vault B exports under the study protocol. |

## The three-phase integration plan

### Phase A (now): curate, don't integrate

The single most valuable asset in the reference Healthcare-AI repository is its structured disease/treatment knowledge CSVs. The integration step is a **data task**, not a systems task:

1. Extract the structured knowledge files (treatments, causes, complications, emergency levels, specialists).
2. **Pharmacist review and curation** — correctness, India-context, patient-appropriateness.
3. Feed the *patient-appropriate subset* into the PMAS vernacular content pipeline ([doc 06](06-multilingual-counselling-content.md)) as background material for counselling authorship — after translation and clinical simplification into the five languages.

### Phase B (thesis period): educational insights, pharmacist-gated

- An optional **"Health Insights"** area in the patient app: symptom-to-education mapping ("based on what you've logged, here is plain-language information about X — this is not a diagnosis; discuss with your pharmacist").
- Any symptom-similarity output is **framed educationally**, carries uncertainty internally (never as a patient-visible percentage), and routes to the pharmacist rather than to a conclusion.
- The reference repo's prediction model — a RandomForest trained on a synthetically-augmented Kaggle symptom dataset — is **not** promoted to patient-facing use, because its evaluation is circular (train and test data generated by the same augmentation process) and its accuracy numbers are not clinically meaningful. If used at all, it is a behind-the-scenes retrieval aid for the pharmacist, clearly labelled.

### Phase C (post-validation, funded): intelligence platform

Only after the pilot study, with a team and funding:

- Retrieval-grounded conversational layer over the curated knowledge base (LLM explains structured content; never invents).
- Model registry + knowledge versioning so every AI output carries reproducibility metadata.
- Pharmacist-facing decision support (adherence-risk flags, counselling prompts) — professional tools, not patient diagnosis.

## Security prerequisites for any AI service

Non-negotiable before an AI service receives a single PMAS request:

- Secrets in environment variables (the reference repo's leaked credentials were rotated and moved before integration work began).
- The AI service receives **de-identified payloads only** — the health-data contract, never Vault A data.
- Every AI interaction logged (input version, model version, output) for audit.
- Rate limits and cost ceilings configured before go-live.

## Integration boundary (reference shape)

```
PMAS backend
   │  POST /health-context  (versioned, de-identified)
   ▼
Intelligence service (separate deployable)
   │  normalise → retrieve structured knowledge → reason → safety-validate
   ▼
Structured response (educational, source-tagged)
   │
   ▼
PMAS presentation layer (5 languages, pharmacist-gated where clinical)
```

The intelligence service is consulted — never depended upon. `PMAS without AI` remains a complete, useful, offline-capable product at every point in this roadmap.
